# The CISO and the Red Square

The final slide glowed against the darkened boardroom wall.

Three red boxes. Five amber. Two green.

The board chair leaned forward. “What does the red one cost us?”

Daniel, the CISO, glanced at the risk analyst beside him. She lowered her eyes to the spreadsheet open on her laptop.

“It means the likelihood is high,” Daniel said.

“I understand the color.” The chair tapped the table. “What does it mean to the bank?”

No one answered.

The red square represented an aging authentication system, two delayed remediation projects, and a possible interruption across forty-three branches. All of that had been compressed into a color chosen from a dropdown menu.

At the next meeting, Daniel did not bring the heatmap.

He placed a quantified exposure report before each director. It showed the estimated annual loss exposure, the assumptions behind it, the affected business units, and the controls expected to reduce it. Every monetary value was preserved to the cent.

The chair studied the first page.

“So this is what we stand to lose?”

“This is our present exposure,” Daniel said. “And this is what changes if we fund the remediation.”

The discussion moved from colors to capital.

For the first time, Daniel was not defending a chart. He was defending a decision.

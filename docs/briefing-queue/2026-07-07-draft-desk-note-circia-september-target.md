> **Signal (7 July 2026):** Reporting on the updated 2026 Unified Agenda preview states CISA expects to issue the final **CIRCIA** (Cyber Incident Reporting for Critical Infrastructure Act) rule in **September 2026**. The same agenda window also projects related federal contracting cybersecurity rules for September. CISA’s June 2026 town halls (reported attendance >1,200 stakeholders) are cited as completed feedback steps.
>
> *Agenda targets are planning signals—not an effective final rule. Statutory clocks (e.g., 72-hour incident / 24-hour ransom payment reporting under the statute’s design) become operational only after a final rule is issued and effective dates apply.*

## What moved

CIRCIA has slipped before. The governance use of this week’s signal is readiness, not celebration: map likely covered-entity criteria from the NPRM, pressure-test incident classification and reporting runbooks against 72-hour / 24-hour style clocks, and inventory where CIRCIA may overlap existing sector reporting (financial, health, energy, etc.).

September 2026 also sits near the reported sunset pressure on Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 liability protections (extended discussions through 30 September 2026 in practitioner analyses)—treat voluntary-sharing legal foundations and mandatory CIRCIA birth as related calendar risk, not identical regimes.

## Governance implication (one test)

1. Name the owner for CIRCIA coverage assessment (legal + cyber + business lines).  
2. Tabletop one “substantial cyber incident” and one ransomware-payment scenario against draft clock assumptions.  
3. Record conflicts with existing regulators’ reporting clocks—do not assume CIRCIA replaces them.

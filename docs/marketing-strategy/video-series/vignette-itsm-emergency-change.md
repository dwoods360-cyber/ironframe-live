# The Head of ITSM and the Emergency Change

The utility’s monitoring wall flashed red just before midnight.

A network device serving a critical operations site had begun dropping connections. The operations team prepared an emergency configuration change while technicians remained on the phone with the vendor.

The change worked.

Service stabilized within fourteen minutes.

Three months later, an auditor asked who had authorized it.

The service-management ticket contained a short note: Emergency fix approved.

The approving manager remembered the call but could not find the message. The vendor had replaced its support portal. A screenshot on a shared drive showed the proposed configuration, but not whether it matched what had been deployed.

The team had restored service. It could not reconstruct the decision.

After that audit, emergency changes were tied to governed evidence records. The request, affected assets, proposed action, approver, implementation record, validation results, and financial impact remained connected from beginning to end. External material passed through a quarantine step before becoming trusted evidence.

The next emergency arrived during a winter storm.

Again, the team moved quickly.

This time, speed did not erase accountability.

When the auditor returned, the head of ITSM opened the change record and followed the chain from the first alarm to the final validation.

Nothing depended on memory.

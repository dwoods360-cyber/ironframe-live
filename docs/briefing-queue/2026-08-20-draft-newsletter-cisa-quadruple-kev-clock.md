> **Signal (20 August 2026):** On Tuesday, CISA added four actively exploited CVEs to the KEV catalog. Federal civilian agencies face a remediation due date of **tomorrow, 21 August 2026**, under BOD 22-01. [1][2] If your “critical = 30 days” policy is still the only clock in the room, the catalog is already ahead of you.
>
> *Companion briefing: Compressed KEV Clocks (queue draft 2026-08-20).*

## What moved

The 18 August add covers Windows IKE (CVE-2026-33824), SharePoint authentication (CVE-2026-55040), vCenter path traversal (CVE-2026-59310), and macOS authentication (CVE-2026-65400). [1] Confirm versions and fixes in the live catalog before declaring closure. [2]

BOD 22-01 makes that due date mandatory for covered federal civilian agencies. Private operators are not automatically on the same statutory clock—but treating KEV as an emergency prioritization signal is how risk committees avoid confusing CVSS severity with active exploitation. [3]

## Governance implication (one test)

1. Can you name which of the four CVEs touch your estate within hours of a KEV add?  
2. Does remediation priority flip when a CVE enters KEV—even if CVSS was already “critical”?  
3. If patching slips past the federal due date (or your emergency SLA), are compensating controls and exposure hours documented for the owner who must report them?

## Do not claim

- That every private company is legally bound to the August 21 FCEB due date  
- Invented daily breach-loss dollars without a model  
- That KEV listing alone equals board negligence in every case  
- Product pitch

# The CFO and the Word “Material”

The incident call began at 2:17 a.m.

A manufacturing system had gone offline. Orders were delayed, two facilities had switched to manual operations, and outside specialists were investigating whether data had been taken.

By sunrise, the chief financial officer had heard the same question from the chief executive, the board chair, and counsel.

“Is it material?”

The technical team described compromised credentials, lateral movement, unavailable servers, and containment progress. Every statement mattered. None answered the financial question.

The CFO began writing figures on a legal pad: hourly production losses, contract penalties, recovery costs, customer attrition, legal expenses, and possible regulatory consequences.

Some estimates overlapped. Others changed depending on how long the outage continued. One spreadsheet rounded values differently from another.

The risk model inside Ironframe brought the assumptions into a single exposure record. Every amount was stored in whole cents. Each scenario showed what was known, what remained uncertain, and how the estimated loss changed as operations returned.

The CFO reviewed the numbers with counsel and the incident team.

“This does not decide materiality for us,” she said. “But now we know what decision we are making.”

The final disclosure judgment remained with management and legal counsel.

It was no longer being made in the dark.

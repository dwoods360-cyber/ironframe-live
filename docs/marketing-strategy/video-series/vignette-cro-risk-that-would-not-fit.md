# The CRO and the Risk That Would Not Fit

Maria, the chief risk officer, moved down the enterprise register one line at a time.

Credit exposure had a dollar value.

Liquidity risk had a dollar value.

Operational interruptions had modeled losses and confidence ranges.

Then she reached cybersecurity.

Risk rating: High.

She looked across the table at the security team.

“How high?”

The security director pointed to the methodology tab. “High means the likelihood and impact both exceed our defined threshold.”

Maria folded her arms. “Would this threaten earnings? Capital reserves? A strategic acquisition?”

“We believe it could.”

“By how much?”

The security director had no number to give her.

Cyber risk remained in a separate language—urgent enough to alarm everyone, but too vague to compare with anything else.

Maria directed the team to rebuild the register around loss exposure. The new model recorded assets, threat frequency, probable loss magnitude, existing controls, and the assumptions beneath each calculation. The figures appeared in the same monetary language used throughout enterprise risk management.

At the next risk committee meeting, cybersecurity no longer occupied a colored box at the edge of the report.

It sat beside credit, liquidity, fraud, and operational resilience.

When the committee shifted funding toward the largest exposure, Maria did not need to argue that cyber belonged in the room.

The numbers had already placed it there.

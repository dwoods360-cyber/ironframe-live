> **Executive Summary:** Australia’s expanded AML/CTF regime applies according to the designated services an entity provides and their geographical link to Australia—not merely the entity’s professional title. Businesses commencing newly regulated services on July 1, 2026 generally had to submit their AUSTRAC enrolment application by July 29, while also implementing an approved ML/TF risk assessment, operational AML/CTF policies, customer-due-diligence and suspicious-matter processes, personnel controls and governing-body oversight. Enrolment, compliance-officer designation and officer notification have related but distinct deadlines. Entities should preserve evidence showing how they scoped their services, approved and implemented their controls, trained relevant personnel and addressed residual risk.
>
> *Information reviewed through August 4, 2026. Originally staged as a June readiness issue; revised after the July 1 commencement and July 29 enrolment application window.*

## I. Exposure Vector

A mid-size professional practice sees “Tranche 2” headlines and assumes either that everyone in the industry is automatically covered or that enrolment alone equals compliance. Both errors create governance failure.

The correct August questions are:

* Do we provide a designated service with a geographical link to Australia—or will we commence one later under the rolling 28-day enrolment rule?
* Has a senior manager approved the entity’s current ML/TF risk assessment and AML/CTF policies, and is the governing body exercising documented ongoing oversight?
* Has an appropriately senior AML/CTF compliance officer been designated, and have the statutory designation and AUSTRAC-notification deadlines been tracked separately?
* Can intake personnel complete initial CDD before providing a designated service (subject to limited exceptions) and escalate suspicious circumstances arising during inquiries—not only after a client is accepted?
* If we commenced newly regulated services on July 1, did we submit our enrolment application by July 29—and if we missed it, have we enrolled immediately and documented residual risk? [1][2][3]

AUSTRAC’s public materials state that newly regulated sectors include real estate, conveyancing, legal services, accounting services, and dealers in precious metals, precious stones and related products. Associated obligations include AML/CTF risk management, customer due diligence, suspicious-matter reporting and recordkeeping. Businesses commencing those newly regulated designated services on July 1, 2026 generally had to **submit** their AUSTRAC enrolment application by July 29, 2026—the deadline to apply, not necessarily the date by which AUSTRAC completed enrolment. [1][2]

**Caution:** Designated services—not job titles—determine whether Tranche 2 applies. A law firm, accounting practice, real-estate business or jeweller is not automatically regulated because of its industry classification. Coverage depends on whether the entity provides a designated service specified by the legislation and whether that service has the required geographical link to Australia. [3][4]

Dealers should test the transaction value, payment method and linked-transaction rules rather than assuming that all jewellery or precious-product sales are regulated. Newly regulated dealer services generally concern purchases or sales of precious metals, precious stones or specified products valued at **A$10,000 or more** where payment involves physical currency or virtual assets (including linked or apparently linked transactions). A dealer that does not accept physical currency or virtual assets for the relevant transactions may not provide that designated service. [5]

## II. Quantitative Context

| Clock | Date / rule | Governance meaning |
| ----- | ----------- | ------------------ |
| Obligation commencement for new designated services | July 1, 2026 | Risk assessment, AML/CTF policies, CDD, training, reporting and oversight duties are live |
| Enrolment application for services commencing July 1 | Generally by July 29, 2026 | Apply within 28 days after commencing a designated service; application ≠ completed enrolment |
| Enrolment after starting a designated service later | Generally within 28 days of starting | Ongoing rule for entities commencing after July 1 [2] |
| Enrolment detail changes | Within 14 days | Keep AUSTRAC Online current [2] |
| Compliance-officer designation (if not already designated at commencement) | Within 28 days after commencement | Distinct from enrolment and from AUSTRAC notification [6] |
| Compliance-officer notification to AUSTRAC | Generally by the later of July 29, 2026 or 14 days after enrolment | An entity applying on July 29 could have until about August 12 to complete notification [6] |
| Independent evaluation | At least once every three years; first evaluation subject to transitional timing | Mandatory under AML/CTF policies; report to governing body and relevant senior manager [7] |

There is no single “industry average fine” that substitutes for entity-specific exposure. The practical quantitative frame for a governing body is: number of designated-service lines in scope; volume of higher-risk clients; time-to-enrol (and late-enrol remediation if applicable); and gaps between written policies and operable controls.

## III. What Modern GRC Must Enforce

### Recommended operating sections for newly regulated entities

| Topic | Minimum readiness evidence |
| ----- | -------------------------- |
| Who is newly regulated | Documented scoping memo: services mapped to designated-service definitions; geographic-link analysis; dealer threshold / payment-method analysis where relevant; out-of-scope rationale |
| AML/CTF program | Senior-manager approval of the risk assessment and AML/CTF policies; documented governing-body oversight of implementation and compliance; ML/TF risk assessment; policies proportionate to risk |
| Compliance officer | Written designation of an officer at management level with sufficient authority, independence and resources; separate tracking of designation and AUSTRAC-notification deadlines |
| Personnel | Relevant personnel trained early enough to perform AML/CTF functions competently from commencement, with role-based initial and ongoing training records retained; personnel due diligence operational before a person begins relevant AML/CTF functions |
| Customer due diligence | Customer identification, beneficial-ownership verification, PEP assessment and targeted-financial-sanctions checks, with enhanced CDD escalation where required; initial CDD generally before providing the service (limited exceptions only) |
| Suspicious-matter reporting | Intake can identify and escalate suspicious circumstances arising during inquiries and onboarding—including before the business agrees to provide the service; ability to lodge required reports via AUSTRAC Online |
| Enrolment and registration | AUSTRAC Online enrolment application under the applicable 28-day rule; registration, in addition to enrolment, may apply where the entity provides registrable remittance or virtual-asset services—check service-specific registration requirements and any staged transitional provisions [2][8] |
| Independent evaluation | Independent-evaluation schedule, including the applicable transitional deadline for the first evaluation and a recurring interval of no more than three years; written report to the governing body and relevant senior manager |
| Governance evidence retained | Risk assessment, senior-manager approvals, governing-body oversight records, training and PDD logs, CDD files, reports, evaluation reports |

### Control translation

| Failure mode | Control requirement |
| ------------ | ------------------- |
| “We are a law firm, so we are in” / “we are out” without analysis | Service-by-service scoping with retained rationale and geographical-link analysis |
| Enrolment without a live program | Treat enrolment and substantive compliance as parallel workstreams |
| Officer named only on a slide | Formal designation within statutory time; AUSTRAC notification tracked separately |
| “All training done by July 1” treated as the statute | Role-based initial and ongoing training timed so staff can perform functions from commencement; retain records |
| Generic “sanctions screening” conflated with all Australian sanctions law | Targeted-financial-sanctions checks within CDD, plus enhanced CDD where risk requires |
| CDD invented after the first suspicious client | Procedures and systems operable at commencement; escalate suspicions arising on inquiry |

Institutional programs consolidating this evidence frequently keep scoping decisions, approvals, and training records in a governed repository so the entity can show what it knew and approved at commencement—not only what it filed after.

## IV. Verification Protocol

1. Confirm AUSTRAC enrolment guidance: apply by July 29, 2026 if providing new designated services commencing July 1, 2026; generally enrol within 28 days of starting a designated service; notify enrolment-detail changes within 14 days. [2]
2. Confirm AUSTRAC’s July 1, 2026 “new reporting regime” announcement describing newly regulated sectors and core obligations. [1]
3. Do not claim automatic coverage of entire professions; verify designated services and geographical link. [3][4]
4. For dealers, verify the A$10,000 / physical-currency / virtual-asset / linked-transaction tests. [5]
5. Confirm Act and AUSTRAC governance rules: senior-manager approval; governing-body oversight; compliance-officer designation and notification; independent evaluation at least every three years. [6][7][9]
6. Keep this newsletter distinct from APRA CPS 230 (prudential operational risk)—different regulator, different perimeter.
7. Remittance and virtual-asset service providers: confirm whether registration applies in addition to enrolment, and check any staged transitional provisions. [2][8]

## Key Takeaways

* July 1 starts substantive obligations; July 29 was the enrolment-application hard date for new designated services commencing that day—not a substitute for the program.
* Entities that commence designated services later remain on the rolling 28-day enrolment rule; entities that missed July 29 should enrol immediately and document residual risk.
* Designated services—not job titles—determine whether Tranche 2 applies.
* Senior-manager approval, governing-body oversight, officer designation/notification and independent evaluation are related but distinct controls.

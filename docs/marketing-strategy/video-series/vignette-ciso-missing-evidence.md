# The CISO and the Missing Evidence

The auditor asked for proof that privileged access had been reviewed during the previous quarter.

Within minutes, six people were searching.

One analyst opened SharePoint. Another searched a service-management platform. A third worked through an email folder labeled “Audit Stuff.” Two spreadsheets appeared with nearly identical filenames.

The first said the review had been completed on March 28.

The second said April 3.

Neither showed who had changed the date.

“Which one is final?” the auditor asked.

The room went still.

By late afternoon, the team had assembled a folder containing screenshots, exported tickets, meeting notes, and an unsigned approval sheet. The control might have operated correctly. The evidence could not prove it.

The following quarter, the access review began inside a control-first workflow. Each request, response, exception, approval, and supporting artifact remained attached to the control it served. When the review closed, the evidence package was sealed.

At the next audit meeting, the same question came.

The analyst opened one record.

The auditor could see who performed the review, what was examined, which exceptions remained open, who approved them, and when each action occurred.

No one searched through email.

No one asked which spreadsheet was final.

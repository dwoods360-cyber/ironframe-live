> **Signal (7 July 2026):** ECB Supervisory Board Chair Claudia Buch issued letter **SSM-2026-0301**, *Addressing AI-enabled cybersecurity threats*, to CEOs of significant institutions. Institutions must assess the evolving threat landscape and submit a comprehensive action plan—concrete controls, resources, roles, timelines—to their Joint Supervisory Team by **31 October 2026**. The letter grounds expectations in **DORA** (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) and calls out short-term priorities: accelerate vulnerability/patch management; enhance monitoring/detection (including AI-enabled defensive capabilities with safeguards); verify third-party ICT risk management for critical supply chains.
>
> Same day: ESRB warning on systemic cyber risks stemming from frontier AI models. ECB also extends the annual IT Risk Questionnaire deadline from September 2026 to **February 2027**.

## What moved

This is not a new AI Act chapter. It is supervisory escalation of **existing DORA ICT resilience duties** under a faster exploitation economy. Management bodies are explicitly on the hook for strategy, investment, risk-tolerance frameworks, and closure of open ICT supervisory findings.

Short-term annex focus includes perimeter and internet-facing assets, third-party/open-source components, change management that can absorb emergency patching, and supply-chain assurance that vendors can keep up with accelerated disclosure. Longer-term asks include defence-in-depth, legacy modernization, and tested response/recovery under high-speed scenarios.

## Governance implication (one test)

1. If you are an ECB significant institution (or a material ICT provider to one), identify the board owner for the 31 October 2026 action plan.  
2. Map the plan’s short-term items to existing DORA ICT risk, incident, testing, and third-party register evidence—not a parallel slide deck.  
3. Record open supervisory ICT findings and whether AI-threat acceleration changes their residual rating.

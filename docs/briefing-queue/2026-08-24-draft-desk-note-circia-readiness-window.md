> **Signal (week of 24 August 2026):** CISA continues to target **September 2026** for the CIRCIA final rule (Unified Agenda planning signal). Covered critical-infrastructure entities are expected, **once the final rule is issued and takes effect**, to report covered cyber incidents to CISA within **72 hours** of reasonably believing a covered incident occurred, and ransom payments within **24 hours** of payment.
>
> *Agenda target ≠ final text or effective date. Prep against the April 2024 NPRM shape; re-check the live CIRCIA RIN on reginfo.gov before treating any month as locked; update playbooks when the final rule publishes.*

## What moved

This is not a re-announcement of the July Unified Agenda desk note. Late August is the practical readiness window: if the final rule lands in September, teams that have not yet mapped likely coverage and reporting owners will be starting under board time pressure—not because the Agenda date is itself a legal deadline. Focus on coverage hypotheses, statutory-style reporting clocks, and **dual-track** obligations (CIRCIA confidential reporting to CISA vs public regimes such as SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05 vs other sector rules). CIRCIA does not replace those other clocks.

## Governance implication (one test)

1. Document a provisional “likely covered / unlikely / unknown” map against NPRM-style sector and size criteria.  
2. Walk the IR playbook against 72-hour incident and 24-hour ransom-payment clocks with named decision makers (trigger → who decides → who files).  
3. Record the owner who will diff final rule vs NPRM within five business days of publication.

## Do not claim

- The Unified Agenda September 2026 date is an enacted statute or a filed final rule  
- CIRCIA reporting clocks are already binding on private entities before the final rule’s effective date  
- CIRCIA replaces SEC Item 1.05, banking, health, or other sector reporting clocks  
- Specific coverage headcount or “you are covered” determinations without NPRM/final-rule criteria applied to the entity

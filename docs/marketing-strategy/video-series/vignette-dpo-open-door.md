# The Data Protection Officer and the Open Door

Elena received the regulator’s request at 8:12 on Monday morning.

The inquiry concerned personal information processed by three subsidiaries and two outside vendors. By noon, her team had collected policies, vendor assessments, screenshots, and access records.

Then she noticed the problem.

A document from the European subsidiary contained notes belonging to the American healthcare business. A vendor reviewer had been able to see evidence from an account that was not theirs. The access had been brief, but the records did not make the boundary clear.

Elena closed her office door.

She did not need another policy declaring that information was segregated. She needed to demonstrate that segregation had actually occurred.

The privacy evidence was moved into isolated entity workspaces. Each subsidiary, vendor, and review team received only the records within its authorized scope. Access decisions were logged. Control evidence remained connected to the entity that produced it.

When the regulator requested additional proof, Elena opened the audit trail.

It showed the control, the responsible entity, the evidence submitted, the reviewers who accessed it, and the exact boundaries that prevented one organization’s records from appearing in another’s workspace.

Elena still made the legal and privacy judgments herself.

Ironframe did not replace her office.

It gave her something policies alone could not provide: a visible locked door.
